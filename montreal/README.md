# Montréal · 3D City

An interactive 3D model of central Montreal in Three.js (r160), for desktop browsers and the Meta Quest 3 (WebXR).

**Live:** https://irk15.github.io/3D-/montreal/

## What's in it

- **Geography**: a 12.8 km × 12.8 km area centred on Place Ville Marie — downtown, Old Montreal and the Old Port, the Plateau, Mile End, Outremont, Westmount, Côte-des-Neiges, Griffintown and the Lachine Canal, Hochelaga and the Olympic Park, Verdun and Nuns' Island, Parc Jean-Drapeau, the Saint Lawrence and the South Shore, with the Monteregian hills on the horizon.
- **Street grid**: the real Montreal grid (rotated 55° from true north, so "Montreal north" points north-west), with arterials (René-Lévesque, Sainte-Catherine, Sherbrooke, Mont-Royal, Saint-Laurent, Saint-Denis, Papineau, Pie-IX…), plex alleys, and Old Montreal's own finer grid.
- **~64,000 buildings** generated by district: plex rows with Montreal's outdoor staircases on the Plateau, greystones, Old Montreal limestone with mansard roofs, downtown towers with podiums and setbacks, Westmount houses, Griffintown condos and warehouses.
- **Landmarks at their real positions**: Place Ville Marie (with its rotating beacon), 1000 de La Gauchetière, Tour de la Bourse, Sun Life, Mary Queen of the World, Notre-Dame Basilica, City Hall, Bonsecours Market, the Clock Tower, La Grande Roue, Silo No. 5 and the Farine Five Roses sign, Habitat 67, the Biosphere, the Casino, La Ronde, the Olympic Stadium and its inclined tower, the Biodome, the Mount Royal Cross, the Chalet and Kondiaronk Belvedere, Saint Joseph's Oratory, Université de Montréal, McGill, Place des Arts, Chinatown, Atwater Market, the port with its cranes, and the Jacques Cartier (with colour-cycling lights), Victoria, Samuel De Champlain and Concorde bridges, plus the REM on its elevated guideway.
- **Life**: signalised traffic with right-hand lanes, one-way side streets, car-following and brake lights; buses; parked cars; bridge traffic; walking pedestrians; gulls; ships on the river.
- **Atmosphere**: physically based sky with clouds, stars and moon; sun position for Montreal's latitude by season; spring/summer/autumn/winter (Mount Royal turns red and gold, snow settles on roofs, river edges freeze); clear, cloudy, rain, snow and fog; height fog; lit windows, street-lamp pools and shopfront signs at night.

## Controls

| Desktop | |
|---|---|
| Orbit (1) | drag to rotate, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom, WASD to glide; click a label for its story |
| Fly (2) | click to capture the mouse, WASD + E/Q, Shift for speed |
| Walk (3) | street level with collisions, Shift to run, Space to jump |
| Keys | N day/night, L labels, U hide the interface, H help |

**Meta Quest 3**: open the link in the Quest Browser and press **Enter VR**. You start on the Kondiaronk Belvedere. Right stick forward and release to teleport (rooftops too), sideways to snap-turn; left stick to walk (grip to sprint, with a comfort vignette). **Y** opens the wrist menu: time, season, weather, teleport to any landmark, the map tablet, comfort and detail settings. Point at a landmark and pull the trigger for its information card. Hand tracking works for pointing and pinching.

URL options: `?q=low|high|ultra`, `?t=21.5` (hour), `?season=winter`, `?weather=snow`, `?tour=0|1|2`, `?lapse`, `?live`.

## No flicker by construction

The VR flicker in earlier projects came from z-fighting. This build removes the causes rather than hiding them:

1. **No stacked surfaces.** Roads, sidewalks, lane markings, crosswalks, alleys, parks, plazas, cobblestones and lamp light pools are all computed in *one* ground shader from baked signed-distance fields and the analytic street grid. Windows, doors, shopfronts and signs live inside the facade shader. There are no decals or overlay planes.
2. **Boxes only touch.** Every building is a stack of axis-aligned boxes that touch but never overlap. An overlap audit runs at load and its result is shown in the Stats panel (it should read `0 ✓`). Faces that share a plane always face opposite ways, so back-face culling removes one of them.
3. **Steep shorelines.** Terrain vertices are snapped onto the shoreline, so water meets steep quays instead of shallow, nearly coplanar slopes; the water also carries a small polygon offset.
4. **Tight depth range.** The near plane grows with altitude on desktop; VR uses near 0.15 m with a fog wall and distance culling. No logarithmic depth buffer.
5. **No shimmer.** Every high-frequency pattern (window grids, markings, cobbles, waves, stars) fades to its average colour using screen-space derivatives. Per-building values use flat varyings, so hashes never sparkle. Shadows are texel-snapped. Window lights are static per window and never re-randomised.
6. **No popping.** Trees, lamps and props dissolve with alpha-to-coverage on the MSAA buffer; far forest is carried by a canopy shell that fades in as the trees fade out; near trees cross-fade to a high-detail model.
7. **Stereo-safe VR.** No screen-space effects in VR (no bloom, SSAO, SSR or depth of field). Water uses sky reflections there; desktop High/Ultra adds a planar mirror.

## Files

- `index.html` — page, HUD and styles
- `js/core.js` — projection, baked masks, street grid, terrain height
- `js/shaders.js` — height fog, ground, facades, water, foliage, glow sprites
- `js/terrain.js`, `js/city.js`, `js/landmarks.js`, `js/vegetation.js`, `js/life.js` — the world
- `js/atmosphere.js`, `js/reflect.js` — sky, light, seasons, weather, water mirror
- `js/controls.js`, `js/xr.js`, `js/ui.js`, `js/main.js` — interaction, VR, interface and the render loop
- `data/mask_a.png`, `data/mask_b.png` — baked map fields; `tools/bake_mask.py` regenerates them

## Sources and accuracy

Borough limits come from the City of Montreal's open data (CC BY 4.0, via Code for America's *click_that_hood*). Shorelines, parks and landmark positions are traced by hand from known coordinates, and buildings are procedural, so this is a faithful impression of Montreal rather than a survey-grade model. Landmark heights and dates follow published figures.
